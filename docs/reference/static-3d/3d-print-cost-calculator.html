What the result means

Total cost per good print is what one sellable print really costs you: filament, power and machine wear, the share of failed prints, your hands-on time, and packaging. Suggested price is the lowest price that still leaves your target margin after the marketplace takes its fees. Price below it and you're paying to make sales.

How to use it

  1. Read filament used and print time from your slicer (Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, Cura and others all show both).
  2. Pick your state to load its average electricity price, or type your exact rate from your bill.
  3. Enter your printer's price and how many hours you expect it to last. Wear is spread across every print hour.
  4. Be honest about failures and hands-on time. Those two usually matter more than filament.
  5. Choose where you sell, set your margin, then use Download CSV to keep a pricing record.

The formula (with a worked example)

material = grams ÷ spool grams × spool price
electricity = watts ÷ 1000 × hours × (¢/kWh ÷ 100)
machine = hours × (printer price ÷ lifetime hours + maintenance/hour)
failure allowance = (material + electricity + machine) ÷ (1 − failure rate) − (material + electricity + machine)
total cost = material + electricity + machine + failure allowance + labour + extras
price = (total cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − margin − fee %)

Worked example (the defaults above): 120 g of PLA from a $22, 1 kg spool = $2.64. 0.12 kW × 6 h × $0.1831 = $0.13 of electricity. 6 h × ($700 ÷ 5,000 h + $0.05) = $1.14 of machine wear. A 10% failure rate adds $0.43, 20 minutes at $15/h adds $5.00, and packaging is $1.50. That's a total cost of $10.85. With Etsy-style fees (9.5% + $0.45) and a 40% margin, price = ($10.85 + $0.45) ÷ (1 − 0.40 − 0.095) = $22.37, which leaves $8.95 profit (40% of the price).

Assumptions and limits

  • Filament density and length estimates assume 1.75 mm filament and typical densities (PLA 1.24, PETG 1.27, ABS 1.04, ASA 1.07, TPU 1.21, nylon 1.14 g/cm³). Brands vary slightly.
  • Power draw varies with bed temperature, enclosure and material. A plug-in power meter gives your real number.
  • Marketplace fee presets are approximations for US sellers and change over time. Check the platform's official fee page and use Custom fees if yours differ. Offsite ads, shipping labels and taxes aren't included.
  • This is an estimate to help you price, not financial or tax advice.

FAQ

How do you calculate the cost of a 3D print?

Add filament (grams used ÷ spool grams × spool price), electricity (kW × hours × rate), machine wear (hours × printer price ÷ lifetime hours), a failed-print allowance, your hands-on time and packaging. The calculator above does it for you and shows each part.

What is a good price to charge for 3D printing?

Start from your full cost per good print, then add a margin that pays for design time, returns and growth. Many sellers aim for 30–50% after fees, but your niche, quality and competition decide what buyers accept. Set the margin field and the calculator works backwards to a price.

How much should I charge per hour of print time?

Print hours mostly cost machine wear and a little electricity: with the defaults, about $0.19 per hour plus $0.02 of power. Your real money is in hands-on minutes and failures, so price those explicitly instead of only charging per print hour.

Is there a 3D print cost calculator for Excel or Google Sheets?

Use Download CSV above. It opens in Excel or Google Sheets with every input and result, so you can keep a pricing log for each product.

How do I calculate the electricity cost of my 3D printer?

Multiply average watts by print hours, divide by 1,000 to get kWh, then multiply by your rate. For example, 120 W × 10 h = 1.2 kWh × $0.1831 = about $0.22. Pick your state above to use its official average.

Can I legally sell 3D prints?

It depends on the design. Prints of your own designs are generally yours to sell. Many downloadable models carry licences (for example, non-commercial Creative Commons) that forbid selling, and trademarked or patented items carry extra risk. Check each model's licence. This isn't legal advice.
